## 1.21.4. Case Study: Engine Emitting Blue Smoke  (Page 1)

**Symptom:** Engine Emitting Blue Smoke

**Machine info:**

| Model   | Machine No.       |   Hours (h) | Location   | Environment   |
|---------|-------------------|-------------|------------|---------------|
| FR60E   | FTC003RLAJX103103 |         920 | Chengdu    | Farm, 20-28°C |

![Repair image 1](images/excavator_repair_case_en_page1_img1.jpeg)

**Key information:**

1. In a case study for an FR60E excavator (Machine No. FTC003RLAJX103103) with 920 hours, the possible causes for the engine emitting blue smoke were identified as potential oil quality issues, insufficient cylinder pressure, or a problem with the fuel injector pump.
2. For the FR60E excavator (Machine No. FTC003RLAJX103103) experiencing an engine emitting blue smoke, the cause analysis pointed to incomplete combustion, indicated by blue smoke and a heavy diesel smell, likely resulting from low cylinder pressure or poor fuel injector atomization.
3. During troubleshooting for the 'Engine Emitting Blue Smoke' case on the FR60E excavator (Machine No. FTC003RLAJX103103), checks confirmed the fuel and high-pressure pump were okay. A cylinder cutout test isolated the issue to cylinder 4, and despite normal cylinder pressure tests, the final diagnosis was a faulty injector that was subsequently replaced.
4. The maintenance plan to resolve the engine emitting blue smoke on the FR60E excavator (Machine No. FTC003RLAJX103103) was to replace the faulty fuel injector.
5. Following the replacement of the fuel injector on the FR60E excavator (Machine No. FTC003RLAJX103103), the fault of the engine emitting blue smoke disappeared, confirming the repair was effective.
6. As a preventative suggestion for the FR60E excavator (Machine No. FTC003RLAJX103103) after resolving the 'Engine Emitting Blue Smoke' case, it is recommended to use high-quality fuel regularly to prevent future injector problems.

**Key information:**

1. In the case of severe idle vibration on an FR-60E excavator (Machine No. FTC003RFVHH10237 8), the possible causes for heavy engine shaking at idle include loose mounting bolts, cylinder misfire, a damaged fuel injector, or worn engine rubber mounts.
2. For the FR-60E excavator with severe idle vibration, the cause analysis determined that loose bolts can cause vibration, misfiring can cause a power imbalance, poor fuel injector atomization can lead to uneven power, and worn engine rubber mounts can fail to absorb engine tremors.
3. Troubleshooting the severe idle vibration on the FR-60E excavator involved noting the vibration was severe under 1500 RPM but smooth otherwise. Technicians tightened all mounting bolts with no change. Specialists tested injectors and for cylinder misfire, finding no issues. An inspection of the engine mounts revealed wear, leading to their replacement.
4. The maintenance plan to resolve the severe idle vibration on the FR-60E excavator was to replace all four engine rubber mounts, which were identified as the root cause of the fault.
5. Following the replacement of the engine rubber mounts on the FR-60E excavator to fix the severe idle vibration, the effect confirmation was that the vibration was eliminated and the engine was running normally.
6. To prevent future occurrences of severe idle vibration on the FR-60E excavator, it is suggested to inspect the condition of the engine rubber mounts during all scheduled maintenance.

**Key information:**

1. In a case study of a High Coolant Temperature Alarm on an FR60E machine (No. FTC003RFTHH10237 3), possible causes for the 120°C alarm and occasional short circuits include a sensor fault, wiring ground, poor engine grounding, a dashboard issue, cooling system blockage, or a slipping fan belt.
2. For the High Coolant Temperature Alarm case on the FR60E machine, the cause analysis involved differentiating between actual engine overheating and faulty sensor readings that could trigger a false alarm.
3. Troubleshooting the High Coolant Temperature Alarm on the FR60E machine revealed it was a false alarm, with the actual temperature measured at 62°C. Although the sensor and wiring tested correctly, a poor connection at the main power switch was identified as the cause of the erratic electrical fault. The fault was cleared after the power switch was replaced.
4. The maintenance plan to resolve the false High Coolant Temperature Alarm on the FR60E machine was to replace the main power master switch, which was identified as the root cause of the electrical fault.
5. Following the replacement of the main power master switch on the FR60E machine, the effect confirmation was that the High Coolant Temperature alarm was successfully fixed.
6. As a preventative measure related to the High Coolant Temperature Alarm case on the FR60E, it is suggested to ensure all grounding points are kept clean and tight to avoid similar electrical faults.

## 1.21.7. Case Study: Occasional Hard Starting After Extended Parking  (Page 4)

**Symptom:** Occasional Hard Starting After Extended Parking

**Machine info:**

| Model   | Machine No.        |   Hours (h) | Location   | Environment          |
|---------|--------------------|-------------|------------|----------------------|
| FR60E   | FTC003RFCLW11005 5 |         413 | Wuhan      | Sand Quarry, 20-28°C |

![Repair image 1](images/excavator_repair_case_en_page4_img1.jpeg)

**Key information:**

1. In case study 1.21.7 for an FR60E machine (S/N FTC003RFCLW11005 5) experiencing occasional hard starting after extended parking, possible causes include a faulty stop solenoid, stop relay, GPS interlock, or a blockage in the low-pressure fuel line.
2. In the case of an FR60E with occasional hard starting after extended parking, the cause analysis revealed that the engine cranks but does not fire, and no smoke is emitted from the exhaust during failed start attempts.
3. Troubleshooting an FR60E with occasional hard starting involved replacing fuel filters, separators, the stop solenoid, and the timer, but the problem persisted. The issue was resolved by cleaning the fuel tank, where rust and water sediment were found at the bottom causing an intermittent fuel flow blockage for the low-pressure pump.
4. For the FR60E machine experiencing occasional hard starting, the prescribed maintenance plan was to deep clean the fuel tank to remove contaminants.
5. After deep cleaning the fuel tank on the FR60E machine, the effect confirmation was that the starting logic was restored, and the hard starting issue was resolved.
6. To prevent future occurrences of hard starting on the FR60E, it is suggested to drain the water separator daily and keep the fuel tank full to avoid condensation buildup.
